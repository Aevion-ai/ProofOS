Long-horizon agents persist state, call tools, modify code, and affect
external systems. Their harnesses increasingly treat a coherent final
response as sufficient evidence that an authorized state transition
occurred. This conflation of \emph{plausible output} with \emph{authorized
transition} is the core defect this paper addresses.

Recent systems make the problem sharper. Prime Agent \cite{prime_agent}
uses a persistent IPython kernel as its primary tool surface; sub-agents are
function calls, sessions detach and recover, and the Continual Harness
treats prompts, agents, skills, and memory as mutable state. Its
\emph{refine} mechanism edits the harness based on the agent's own
trajectory. Cloudflare WriteGuard \cite{writeguard} centralizes policy,
attribution, and audit for MCP writes, classifying tools into risk tiers and
blocking actions before tool handlers execute; Cloudflare explicitly states
it does not trust client-side skills or prompts. A machine-checked
resume-contract study \cite{resume_contract} defines six properties for
workflow persistence---prefix continuation, effect exactly-once, fork
determinism, checkpoint validity, consume-once, and recovery
determinism---and found materially different failure behavior among five
agent workflow frameworks, including repeated effects after crashes and
concurrent resume racers firing the same gated effect multiple times.

These systems validate three premises: (i) the harness itself can evolve,
(ii) centralized write control is necessary, and (iii) persistence semantics
require formal guarantees. What remains open is the authority question. A
self-modifying harness may alter candidate memory, skills, prompts, and
delegation structure; it must not be able to alter the authority conditions
under which those changes become operational.

We contribute a transition algebra that binds compiled context,
authenticated principal, independent authority root, effect-bounded
execution, typed obligations, content-bound receipts, checkpoint/resume
semantics, and candidate-only reflection into one fail-closed gate.

\paragraph{Contribution summary.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Typed state-transition obligations.} A consequential
  transition $x_t \xrightarrow{a_t} x_{t+1}$ requires discharge of
  obligations for identity, authority, evidence, ACP binding, XAL
  verification, continuity, and effect scope. Missing any mandatory
  obligation forces HOLD or REJECT, never ADMIT.
  \item \textbf{Reject absorption.} Once a hard fault exists, downstream
  components cannot convert the transition into ADMIT, PASS, or PROMOTE.
  \item \textbf{Candidate-only self-evolution.} Reflection, memory
  compilers, and self-improvement emit candidates; they cannot produce
  authority. Same-epoch self-certification is excluded.
  \item \textbf{Receipt-bound replay and resume semantics.} A receipt binds
  execution graph, XAL program, authority lease, environment, input,
  effects, and result; digest divergence invalidates replay. A consumed
  resume cannot fire twice; an invalid checkpoint cannot resume.
  \item \textbf{Independent authority roots.} Two identities derived from
  the same authority, credential, model-session, or campaign root do not
  constitute an independent reviewer or quorum.
\end{enumerate}
