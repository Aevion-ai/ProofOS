\begin{abstract}
Long-horizon agents increasingly persist state, call tools, modify code, and
affect external systems, yet most harnesses treat a coherent final response
as sufficient evidence that an authorized state transition occurred. We
introduce No Witness, No Verdict, a fail-closed architecture in which every
consequential transition must discharge typed obligations for identity,
authority, evidence admissibility, program and environment binding,
continuity, and side-effect scope. The architecture compiles an explainable
execution graph through a bounded planning pass into an assembly-like
execution representation, emits content-bound receipts, and separates
reflection from promotion: self-evolution may generate candidates but cannot
grant authority. We formalize reject absorption, candidate-only non-authority,
same-epoch self-certification exclusion, independent-authority requirements,
resume semantics (a consumed resume cannot fire twice), and receipt-bound
replay. A reference implementation is evaluated against adversarial
transition fixtures covering self-asserted capability, evidence laundering,
route/action mismatch, receipt tampering, same-root review, checkpoint
invalidity, and execution failure after valid admission. The released
artifact includes source, schemas, formal statements, tests, and explicit
claim ceilings. Results apply only to the released configuration and do not
constitute product or regulatory certification.
\end{abstract}
